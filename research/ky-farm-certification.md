# Getting "Certified" as a Farm in Kentucky — Research Report

*Prepared September 2026. Not legal or tax advice. Confirm details with your county PVA, the Kentucky Department of Revenue, your local USDA Service Center, and a farm-savvy CPA before acting.*

---

## 1. The short answer

Kentucky has **no single "farm certificate."** Being recognized as a farm is really a set of separate registrations, each run by a different agency, each with its own rules and payoff. Most farms need three of them:

| # | Registration | Who runs it | What it gets you | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Agriculture Exemption (AE) Number** | KY Department of Revenue (DOR) | No 6% sales tax on qualifying farm purchases | Free |
| 2 | **Agricultural / Horticultural property assessment** | Your county PVA (Property Valuation Administrator) | Land taxed at its farm "use value," far below market value | Free |
| 3 | **USDA FSA Farm Number** | Your county USDA Service Center | Access to federal programs, loans, disaster aid, and most state cost-share | Free |

Optional add-ons worth knowing about: Kentucky Proud membership, Agricultural District enrollment, farm truck plates, organic certification, and agritourism protections (Section 4).

**Recommended order:** FSA farm number first, then the AE number, then the property-tax application. The FSA number is one of the documents the other two accept as proof you farm.

---

## 2. What counts as a "farm"? (Eligibility)

Each program uses its own test. The main thresholds:

| Program | Minimum land | Activity test |
|---|---|---|
| AE Number (sales tax) | None stated | "Regularly engaged" in farming **as a business**: growing crops, raising livestock for food, dairy, poultry, or raising ratites, llamas, alpacas, bison, cervids, or aquaculture. Proven by tax/FSA documents. |
| Property tax: **agricultural land** | **10 contiguous acres** | Used to produce livestock, poultry, their products, tobacco, other crops, or timber |
| Property tax: **aquaculture** | **5 contiguous acres** | Commercially used for aquaculture |
| Property tax: **horticultural land** | **5 contiguous acres** | Commercially used for gardens, orchards, fruit/nuts, vegetables, flowers, or ornamental plants |
| FSA Farm Number | None | You must show control of land (deed, lease, or rental agreement) |
| Zoning "agricultural use" (KRS 100.111) | **5 contiguous acres** (generally) | Exempts farm use from most zoning rules |

**Key takeaway:** Under 5 acres, you can likely still get an AE number and FSA number, but you will usually **not** get the property-tax break. Between 5 and 10 acres, only horticulture or aquaculture qualifies for the property-tax break.

## 3. Step-by-step process

### Step 1 — Get a USDA FSA Farm Number (1–2 weeks)

1. Find your county USDA Service Center (farmers.gov "Service Center Locator") and book an appointment.
2. Bring:
   - Photo ID and Social Security number or EIN (Employer Identification Number — a business tax ID).
   - Proof of land control: recorded deed, or a written lease/rental agreement.
   - If you farm as an LLC, trust, or partnership: articles of organization, trust documents, or partnership agreement.
3. You'll complete these forms with FSA staff:
   - **AD-2047** — Customer Data Worksheet (your contact info).
   - **CCC-902** — Farm Operating Plan (who owns/operates what).
   - **AD-1026** — Highly Erodible Land & Wetland Conservation certification (required for most USDA money).
   - **CCC-941** — Adjusted Gross Income certification (needed for many payment programs).
4. FSA locates the parcel on its maps and assigns a farm number. **It's free.**

Note: the farm number stays with the **land**, not with you. If you sell or stop leasing, the number stays with the parcel.

### Step 2 — Get a Kentucky Agriculture Exemption (AE) Number (2–6 weeks)

Since **January 1, 2023** (KRS 139.481, as amended by 2022 SB 121), a farmer **must** have an AE number to buy farm inputs tax-free. The old "just sign the form" approach no longer works.

1. Fill out **Form 51A800, Application for Agriculture Exemption** (online through the DOR website, or paper).
2. Describe your farm activity in detail (what you grow or raise, and that it's for sale).
3. Attach at least one proof document. DOR accepts:
   - **IRS Schedule F** (Profit or Loss from Farming) — *DOR says this is the fastest option.*
   - **IRS Form 4835** (Farm Rental Income).
   - **FSA Farm Number** (from Step 1).
   - Other verification DOR accepts (e.g., entity tax returns showing farm income).
4. Submit online, by email to DOR's sales tax division, or by mail to: Division of Sales and Use Tax, Station 66, P.O. Box 181, Frankfort, KY 40602-0181.
5. DOR mails you a letter with your AE number.
6. **Renew every 3 years.** The number expires 3 years after it is issued.

**How you use it:** When you buy, you give the seller a signed certificate with your AE number:
- **Form 51A158, Farm Exemption Certificate** — for feed, seed, fertilizer, farm machinery and repair parts, fuel for farm machinery, baling twine/wire, etc.
- **Form 51A159, On-Farm Facilities Certificate** — for building materials and equipment for barns, poultry/livestock/dairy facilities, grain bins, and similar.

The AE number does **not** make *everything* tax-free. The item must be used "exclusively and directly" in farming. A truck, a lawn mower for the house, household fuel, or tools you also use for non-farm work generally do **not** qualify.

### Step 3 — Apply for Agricultural/Horticultural Property Assessment

1. Get **Form 62A351** (Application for Valuation, Assessment and Taxation of Land under the Agricultural and Horticultural Land Use Act) from your county PVA office or website.
2. You must **own the land as of January 1** of the tax year.
3. **File by March 1** to get the lower valuation for that year.
4. The PVA may ask for proof of farm use: FSA farm number, Schedule F, lease to a farmer, photos, or a site visit. Requirements vary by county.
5. Once approved, you generally stay in the program as long as the use doesn't change, but the PVA can re-check at any time.

### Step 4 — Meet ongoing obligations

- **Agriculture Water Quality Plan.** Kentucky's Agriculture Water Quality Act (1994) requires every landowner with **10+ acres** in farm or timber use to have and follow a water quality plan. Your county Conservation District can help, often for free. This plan is also required for many cost-share programs (like CAIP).
- **Keep records.** Keep receipts and exemption certificates for **at least 4 years** (Kentucky's audit window).
- **Renew the AE number** every 3 years.
- **Report land-use changes** to the PVA within **90 days** (see Risks).

## 4. Optional registrations worth considering

| Program | What it is | Key requirements | Why you might want it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kentucky Proud** | State marketing brand, run by KY Dept. of Agriculture (KDA) | Sell Kentucky-grown or processed ag products; primary business in KY. **Free**, apply online year-round. | Use the logo, get listed in directories, access promotional cost-share grants. |
| **Agricultural District** (KRS 262.850) | Group of farms, 250+ contiguous acres total, enrolled voluntarily through the county Conservation District | Neighbors can combine land to reach 250 acres. | Land **can't be annexed** by a city, gets protection from condemnation, deferred water-line assessments, extra points on state cost-share and PACE easement applications. |
| **County Agricultural Investment Program (CAIP)** | State cost-share (tobacco settlement money) for farm improvements | Kentucky resident, FSA farm number, water quality plan, one applicant per household. | Reimburses part of the cost of fencing, barns, equipment, etc. |
| **Farm truck plates** (KRS 186.050) | Reduced-fee registration at the county clerk | Truck ≤ 38,000 lbs; you sign that you're a farmer; **no for-hire hauling**. | Registration around $11.50/year instead of standard truck fees. |
| **Organic certification** | Kentucky Dept. of Agriculture is a USDA-accredited certifier | 3 years without prohibited substances on the land; annual inspection. About **$500/year** fee. | Price premium; USDA reimburses up to 75% of cert costs (up to $750 per category) when funded. |
| **Agritourism protections** (KRS 247.800–.810) | Limited liability for farm tours, u-pick, corn mazes, etc. | Post an 18"×24" warning sign or get signed waivers. | Protects you from lawsuits over "inherent risks" of farm visits. |

## 5. Benefits

### Financial
1. **Sales-tax savings (6%).** On $20,000/year of feed, seed, fertilizer, and parts, that's about **$1,200/year**. On a $60,000 tractor, **$3,600** saved at once.
2. **Property-tax savings.** Farmland is taxed on what it's worth *as farmland*, not what a developer would pay. Near growing towns, this can cut the land portion of the bill by **50–90%**.
3. **Access to federal money.** The FSA farm number unlocks FSA loans (including beginning-farmer loans with low rates), crop insurance, disaster payments, and NRCS conservation cost-share (EQIP, CSP).
4. **State cost-share.** CAIP and other Kentucky Agricultural Development Fund programs often reimburse 50% of qualifying improvements.
5. **Cheaper vehicle registration** with farm plates.

### Legal and practical
6. **Zoning exemption.** On 5+ acres in agricultural use, most local zoning rules don't apply (except setbacks, flood plains, and dwelling rules).
7. **Right-to-Farm protection** (KRS 413.072). Once an operation has run for **more than 1 year** and wasn't a nuisance at start, neighbors who move in later generally can't sue over normal farm noise, smell, or dust — as long as you're not negligent.
8. **Annexation protection** if you join an Agricultural District.
9. **Credibility.** Official status helps with lenders, suppliers, and marketing (Kentucky Proud logo).

## 6. Risks and downsides

### Tax risks (most important)

1. **Rollback taxes when land stops being farmed.**
   Under KRS 132.454, if you convert agricultural land to another use (sell to a developer, build a subdivision, stop farming), you owe the **tax difference between farm value and full market value for the current year plus the two prior years** — roughly **three years of "saved" taxes, all at once.** You must report the change to the PVA within **90 days**. This matters most if you might sell to a developer.

2. **Misusing the AE number.**
   If you buy tax-free items that don't qualify (personal-use items, home fuel, a truck, non-farm tools), you personally owe the tax **plus interest and penalties** (KRS 139.990). DOR can audit back **4 years**. The number is tied to you, and sellers can look it up in a state database.

3. **IRS "hobby farm" scrutiny.**
   Filing a Schedule F with repeated losses draws attention. Under IRS Section 183, a farm is *presumed* to be a business if it shows a profit in **3 of 5 years** (2 of 7 for horses). If the IRS decides it's a hobby, you **lose the ability to deduct farm losses** against other income and may owe back taxes. Since the AE number often depends on a Schedule F, a weak Schedule F can hurt both.

4. **Losing eligibility.** If you stop farming "as a business," your AE number can be denied at renewal, and the PVA can remove the agricultural valuation.

### Compliance burden
5. **Paperwork.** Three separate agencies, renewals every 3 years, receipts for 4 years, and water quality plan upkeep.
6. **USDA conservation rules.** Signing AD-1026 means you agree to follow wetland and highly-erodible-land rules. Breaking them (for example, draining a wetland) can make you **ineligible for all USDA benefits and require repayment**.
7. **Program-specific obligations.** Cost-share contracts (CAIP, EQIP) usually require you to keep the improvement in place for years; selling early can mean repaying funds.

### Other risks
8. **Agricultural District limits.** Enrolling is voluntary but lasts a set term; leaving early or developing land has consequences, and it can limit near-term development plans.
9. **Farm-plate limits.** Using a farm-plated truck for hire or non-farm business risks fines and loss of the plate.
10. **Liability exposure.** Being an active farm (especially with visitors, employees, or livestock) raises liability risk. Protections like agritourism limits only apply if you follow the rules exactly (signs/waivers). A farm liability insurance policy is strongly recommended.
11. **Privacy.** Registrations create public or semi-public records tying you to the land and business (the AE number database, PVA records).

## 7. Risk vs. benefit — quick decision guide

| Your situation | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Actively farming, selling product, 10+ acres | **Do all three.** Benefits clearly outweigh risks. |
| Farming for sale on under 5 acres | Get the **FSA and AE numbers**. Property-tax break likely unavailable. |
| 5–10 acres of vegetables, fruit, or flowers sold commercially | Get all three; apply as **horticultural** land. |
| Mainly a home with some animals, little or no sales | **Be cautious.** You likely don't meet "as a business." Claiming exemptions risks back taxes and penalties. |
| Might sell land to a developer within a few years | Property-tax savings are real but plan for **3-year rollback**. Budget for it. |
| Leasing land to a neighbor who farms it | You can usually still get the property-tax valuation (land is in farm use). The **farmer** (tenant) usually holds the AE number. Form 4835 supports your case. |

## 8. Checklist to get started

- [ ] Book an appointment at the county USDA Service Center; bring ID, deed/lease, and entity documents.
- [ ] Complete AD-2047, CCC-902, AD-1026, CCC-941; receive FSA farm number.
- [ ] Contact the county Conservation District; complete an Agriculture Water Quality Plan (required at 10+ acres).
- [ ] File Form 51A800 with DOR, attaching Schedule F and/or FSA farm number.
- [ ] Put an AE-number renewal reminder on your calendar for 3 years out.
- [ ] Get Form 62A351 from the county PVA; file **by March 1**.
- [ ] Set up a simple record system: keep all receipts and exemption certificates for 4+ years.
- [ ] Talk with a CPA about Schedule F and the hobby-loss rules.
- [ ] Optional: Kentucky Proud application, farm truck plates, CAIP application (check county deadlines, often spring).
- [ ] Get farm liability insurance.

## 10. Items to verify locally

These points could not be fully confirmed from primary sources during this research. Confirm them before relying on them:

1. **2025 HB 610** proposed repealing and re-enacting KRS 132.010 (the statute that defines agricultural land). Ask your PVA whether any 2025–2026 changes affect acreage or eligibility definitions.
2. **The exact list of documents DOR accepts** for the AE number beyond Schedule F, Form 4835, and FSA number. Check the current Form 51A800.
3. **County-level proof requirements** for the PVA application vary. Some PVAs ask for income evidence or inspect the land.
4. **Organic certification fees and USDA cost-share rates** change year to year based on funding.
